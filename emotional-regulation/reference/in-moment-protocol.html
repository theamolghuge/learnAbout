The In-Moment Protocol

One page, print it, keep it somewhere you'll actually see it. From Lesson 2.

Trigger → activation → labeling → pause → regulation → perspective → options → consequences → deliberate action

10 seconds — mid-conversation, no privacy

  • "[Emotion], noted."
  • One physiological sigh: short inhale — top-up inhale — long exhale out the mouth.
  • "I don't have to respond yet."

2 minutes — you can step aside

  • "Where is this in my body?" (10s)
  • "This is [emotion], at about [0–10]." (10s)
  • Three physiological sighs — nothing else yet. (20s)
  • "What would I think if a friend described this exact situation to me?" (30s)
  • "What are three different things I could do right now?" — force three. (40s)
  • "Given tomorrow-me, which of these?" (10s)

20 minutes — it actually matters

  • Run the 2-minute version first, in full.
  • "What information am I actually missing here?"
  • "What am I tempted to do purely to make this discomfort stop right now?"
  • "If this intensity were half of what it is, what would I choose?"
  • "What will future-me, a week from now, wish I'd done?"
  • "Am I moving toward something I actually want, or just away from this feeling?"
  • Write the answers down if you can.

Leave-and-return — too activated for any of the above to land

Say to the other person: "I want to keep talking about this, and I'm too activated right now to do it well. Give me [a concrete time] and I'll come back to it."

Once alone: cold water on face/wrists, or a short hard burst of exertion. Paced breathing until intensity ≤ 4/10.

Before returning: re-run the 2-minute version. Then actually go back — a leave that never becomes a return is avoidance, not regulation.

The one rule if you forget everything else Label before you act. Everything else on this page is what to do with the gap that creates.
