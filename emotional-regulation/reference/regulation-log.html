Regulation Log

Fill this in after a real incident, not a hypothetical one. From Lesson 6.

Copy the eleven fields below into notes, a doc, or print this page and fill it by hand. The point of the fixed order is that it forces specific reflection ("that action didn't work, here's the alternative") instead of vague self-judgment ("I'm bad at this"). Answer every field about the specific incident — no generalizing mid-entry.

1. Trigger
What specifically happened, right before the reaction — the external event, not your interpretation of it yet.
2. Emotion
Name it as precisely as you can — not just "bad." (See emotional granularity — precise labels open up precise responses.)
3. Intensity (0–10)
At the peak, not at the end.
4. Body sensations
Where you felt it physically — the earliest signal, per Lesson 1.
5. Automatic thought
The story your mind generated in the moment — usually a prediction or judgment, not a fact.
6. Urge
What you felt like doing, before you necessarily did it.
7. Action
What you actually did — separate this from the urge even if they matched.
8. Immediate consequence
What happened in the next few minutes.
9. Delayed consequence
What happened hours or days later — often different from the immediate one, and often the one that actually mattered.
10. What I wish I'd done
Specific and actionable — not "been calmer," but the actual alternative action.
11. What I'll try next time
One concrete thing — ideally tied to a stage of the in-moment protocol (e.g. "label it earlier," "use the 2-minute version instead of skipping straight to reacting").
After a few entries Look across them, not just at one. Recurring trigger, recurring automatic thought, or a consistent gap between "what I wish I'd done" and "what I'll try next time" not actually changing — any of those is worth raising with your coach directly. That's how this feeds back into your dysregulation map and, eventually, a learning record.
