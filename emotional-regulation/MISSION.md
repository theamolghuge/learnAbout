# Mission: Emotional Regulation Under Stress

## Why
You want to be able to feel intense emotional states — anxiety, anger, fear, shame, urgency, excitement — without those states making your decisions for you. This isn't about feeling less. It's about the moments where emotional intensity currently degrades your judgment (arguments, rejection, criticism, uncertainty, fear of loss, high-stakes decisions) becoming moments where you can still act deliberately.

**Status: confirmed at this level of detail (2026-08-13).** The user arrived with an unusually complete specification of both the mental model and the teaching method (see [[NOTES.md]] for the full method — interview cadence, simulation format, protocol structure, log format, rationalization checklist). That spec is treated as authoritative for *how* to teach; this file tracks *what* has actually been established so far.

## Success looks like
- Can articulate, in their own words and with their own examples, the difference between feeling an emotion, expressing it, and acting because of it.
- Has a personal dysregulation map — real triggers, body signals, recurring thoughts, impulsive behaviors, avoidance patterns — built from their own disclosed answers, not assumed by the coach.
- Can produce the in-moment protocol (10-second / 2-minute / 20-minute / leave-and-return versions) under a realistic simulated activation, not just recite it on request.
- Given "I feel X," can generate at least 3 candidate actions before picking one, and can label a chosen response as "emotionally understandable" vs. "strategically wise" — and explain when those two diverge.
- Keeps a regulation log after real incidents (trigger → emotion → intensity → body → automatic thought → urge → action → consequences → what they'd try next time) and can name their own recurring escalation pattern from it, unprompted.
- Progress is measured by shorter escalation, earlier recognition, faster recovery, and fewer impulsive actions — not by the absence of strong emotion.

## Constraints
- This is coaching, not therapy. If a scenario or disclosure suggests serious mental-health symptoms (self-harm, dissociation, trauma responses beyond ordinary stress) or an unsafe/coercive situation, say so explicitly and separate that from the coaching in progress — don't coach through it as if it were a garden-variety regulation problem.
- Regulation is never a justification for tolerating abuse, coercion, or a genuine boundary violation. When a scenario involves one, the "wise" answer may be to escalate or leave, not to self-regulate and stay.
- The coach does not auto-validate rationalizations ("I had no choice," "they deserved it," "that's just how I am") — see the checklist in [[NOTES.md]]. Compassionate, but intellectually demanding.
- Practice should mostly be things doable in ordinary daily life (5–15 min), not exercises requiring a special environment.

## Out of scope
- Diagnosing or treating clinical conditions (panic disorder, PTSD, mood disorders). If these surface, name the boundary and suggest professional support rather than coaching around it.
- Generic "EQ" / positivity content with no mechanism behind it.
