# Emotional Regulation Resources

## Knowledge

- [Chapter: "Process Model of Emotion Regulation" — Psychology of Human Emotion (Open Access Textbook)](https://psu.pb.unizin.org/psych425/chapter/process-model-of-emotion-regulation/), grounded in [McRae & Gross, "Emotion Regulation" (2020)](https://digitalcommons.du.edu/psychology_faculty/203/)
  James Gross's process model: emotion regulation strategies cluster into five families — situation selection, situation modification, attentional deployment, cognitive change, response modulation — occurring at different points in the emotion-generation timeline. Antecedent-focused strategies (early, before the emotion fully forms) reliably outperform response-focused ones (after it's already underway). Use for: the backbone of "why early intervention beats willpower after the fact," and for classifying which stage a given technique (breathing, reappraisal, distraction) actually targets.
- [Gross, J.J. & John, O.P. (2003), "Individual Differences in Two Emotion Regulation Processes," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/10614544_Individual_Differences_in_Two_Emotion_Regulation_Processes_Implications_for_Affect_Relationships_and_Well-Being) — [Emotion Regulation Questionnaire (ERQ), original scale](https://spl.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj19321/files/media/file/english_0.pdf)
  The landmark study distinguishing cognitive reappraisal (reinterpreting a situation before it fully lands) from expressive suppression (inhibiting the outward expression after the fact). Reappraisal is associated with better mood, better relationships, and better well-being; suppression is associated with worse of all three despite "looking calm." Use for: the empirical case that *how* you regulate matters, not just *whether* you look composed — and for distinguishing genuine regulation from suppression that only manages appearance.
- [Lieberman, M.D. et al. (2007), "Putting Feelings Into Words: Affect Labeling Disrupts Amygdala Activity in Response to Affective Stimuli," *Psychological Science*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01916.x) — [UCLA press summary](https://www.uclahealth.org/news/release/putting-feelings-into-words-produces-therapeutic-effects-in-the-brain-ucla-neuroimaging-study-supports-ancient-buddhist-teachings)
  fMRI evidence that naming an emotion ("I feel angry") measurably reduces amygdala reactivity and engages right ventrolateral prefrontal cortex — the mechanism behind "name it to tame it." Use for: why the labeling step in the in-moment protocol isn't just a platitude, it's a specific, replicated neural effect.
- [Siegel, D.J., *The Developing Mind* — Window of Tolerance overview, NICABM](https://www.nicabm.com/trauma-how-to-help-your-clients-understand-their-window-of-tolerance/) and [Psychology Tools summary](https://www.psychologytools.com/resource/window-of-tolerance)
  A zone of physiological arousal within which a person stays regulated, cognitively flexible, and able to think clearly; above it is hyperarousal (fight/flight — anger, panic), below it is hypoarousal (freeze/shutdown — numbness, dissociation). Use for: a shared vocabulary for "activated" that separates the *direction* of dysregulation (too hot vs. too flat), since the fix differs by direction.
- [Balban, M.Y. et al. (2023), "Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal," *Cell Reports Medicine*](https://www.cell.com/cell-reports-medicine/fulltext/S2666-3791(22)00474-8) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36630953/))
  RCT (n=114, 28 days) comparing cyclic sighing, box breathing, cyclic hyperventilation, and mindfulness meditation. Cyclic sighing (double inhale through the nose, long exhale through the mouth) produced the largest drop in respiratory rate and the biggest mood improvement, measurable after a single 5-minute session. Use for: the one physiological technique in this workspace with the strongest direct RCT evidence — the 10-second protocol leans on this.
- [TIPP Skill — Dialectical Behavior Therapy (Linehan)](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/distress-tolerance/tipp/) and [STOP Skill](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/emotion-regulation/stop-skill/)
  DBT's crisis-survival skills for when arousal is too high to think: Temperature (cold water/ice on the face triggers the mammalian dive reflex, dropping heart rate within seconds), Intense exercise, Paced breathing, Paired muscle relaxation — plus the four-step STOP (Stop / Take a step back / Observe / Proceed mindfully) as the "gateway" pause skill. Use for: the fastest tier of the in-moment protocol, for when the situation is too hot for anything cognitive to land yet.
- [Loewenstein, G., "Hot-cold empathy gaps and medical decision making" — overview via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-cold_empathy_gap) and [The Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/empathy-gap)
  In a "hot" (visceral, activated) state, people systematically underestimate how much that state is driving their judgment — and in a "cold" state, they underestimate how differently they'll act once hot. Use for: explaining *why* self-awareness specifically fails at the moment it's needed most, and why decisions made while hot need a deliberate "what would I choose 50% less intense" check rather than trusting introspection in the moment.
- [Feldman Barrett, L. — Emotional Granularity](https://lisafeldmanbarrett.com/category/emotional-granularity/) ([Wikipedia overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emotional_granularity))
  People who make finer distinctions between emotional states ("disappointed" vs. "humiliated" vs. "resentful," not just "bad") show better emotion regulation and physical health outcomes — vague labels drive vague, reflexive coping; precise labels open up precise, chosen responses. Use for: the vocabulary-building side of the labeling step — the "10-second" protocol version encourages a fast label, this is what makes labels sharper over time.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [r/DBTSelfHelp](https://reddit.com/r/DBTSelfHelp)
  Practitioner-run community built around DBT distress-tolerance and emotion-regulation skills specifically (not general mental health venting). Use for: troubleshooting a skill that isn't landing in real situations, seeing how others adapt TIPP/STOP to their own triggers.
