Teaching workspace

Emotional Regulation Under Stress

Feeling anxiety, anger, fear, shame, urgency, or excitement fully — without letting the state make the decision for you.

Mission: act deliberately under emotional load, not feel less. See MISSION.md. This is coaching, not therapy — see the constraints section of the mission for where that line sits.

Lessons

Work through these in order — each builds on the last.

Lesson 1 · ~6 min
What Emotional Dysregulation Actually Is
Feel / express / act as three separable things, why activation degrades exactly the self-awareness you'd need to catch it, and regulation vs. suppression/numbing. Hands off to a live interview in chat.
Lesson 2 · ~8 min
The In-Moment Protocol
Trigger → activation → labeling → pause → regulation → perspective → options → consequences → deliberate action, at four time budgets (10 seconds, 2 minutes, 20 minutes, leave-and-return), with exact self-talk for each. Includes a live 2-minute practice timer.
Lesson 3 · ~10 min · Interactive
The Pause Practice
Why "I feel X" and "therefore I do Y" are separate sentences, a reflex/opposite/delay structure for generating three real options instead of one, and six retrieval-practice prompts (angry, afraid, rejected, uncertain, ashamed, excited).
Lesson 4 · ~9 min
Deciding While Activated
"This emotion contains information" vs. "this emotion should control my behavior." The hot-cold empathy gap, and a 7-question checklist for real decisions made under load.
Lesson 5 · ~8 min
Challenging Your Rationalizations
Eight sentences that show up after an emotionally-driven action ("I had no choice," "that's just how I am"...), each sometimes true — and the question that tells the honest case from the post-hoc one.
Lesson 6 · ~9 min
Recovery, Not Perfection
What progress actually looks like (shorter escalation, earlier recognition, faster recovery — never "feels less"), a repair structure that doesn't smuggle in a justification, and returning to a conversation you left.
Lesson 7 · ~15 min · Interactive
Simulations I: Interpersonal Heat
Six scenarios — insult mid-argument, a loved one growing distant, unfair criticism, an angry message, feeling ignored, rejection. Write your real reaction before revealing the "understandable vs. wise" analysis.
Lesson 8 · ~15 min · Interactive
Simulations II: Stakes and Desire
Six more — wanting to quit after a bad day, jealousy, a threat to leave, fear of missing an opportunity, desperately wanting an uncertain outcome, shame after a mistake.
Lesson 9 · ~8 min + ongoing
The Practice Ladder
Nine rungs of progressive daily practice, low-stakes irritation through high-stakes decisions, each a real 5–15 minute exercise in ordinary life — no special environment required.
Where this leaves things Every section of the original curriculum that could be taught as general-skill content is now built (Lessons 1–9). What's left is exactly the part that can't be pre-generated: your personal dysregulation map — real triggers, body signals, and patterns — which only comes from actually answering the interview question still open since Lesson 1, and from working the practice ladder above and bringing real regulation-log entries back.
