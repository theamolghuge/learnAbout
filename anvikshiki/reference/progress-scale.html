Progress Scale

Six dimensions, each anchored to something observable — a journal entry, a specific catch, a specific correction — not a feeling of having "gotten better."

The original request for this course asked for a 1-10 scale across a dozen separate measures. Twelve separate ten-point scales would mostly produce indistinguishable adjacent levels — padding, not signal. These six composite dimensions cover the same ground, each anchored at four checkable points instead of ten vague ones.

1. Real-time pause

1Acts on impulse before noticing there was a gap to use at all.
4Notices the gap after acting, in hindsight, sometimes.
7Runs "feel it, name it" (Lesson 9) in the moment on at least half of strong reactions, checkable against real-time notes.
10The full "feel it, name it, check it, then act" sequence runs by default on charged moments, evidenced by journal entries written the same day, not reconstructed later.

2. Fact vs. interpretation / assumption detection

1Interpretations are reported and acted on as if they were direct observations.
4Can separate the two when asked directly, but doesn't do it unprompted.
7Belief journal entries routinely name the pramana (Lesson 2) without being prompted to.
10Catches and names a fact/interpretation fusion in someone else's claim, or one's own, within a live conversation — not just in a written journal after the fact.

3. Inference quality

1Accepts the first plausible explanation; never searches for a counter-example.
4Searches for counter-examples when reminded to, using the checklist.
7Journal entries show a genuine counter-example search (Lesson 3) that changed the conclusion at least once in the past month.
10Identifies a specific hetvabhasa or modern bias (Lessons 4, 10, 11, 15) by name, in real time, in one's own reasoning, without prompting.

4. Uncertainty tolerance and belief revision

1Treats "I don't know yet" as a failure to be avoided; forces a conclusion either way.
4Can state "suspended, pending X" when asked to, but defaults to a forced verdict otherwise.
7At least one journal entry in the past month ends in an honest, named suspension (satpratipaksha, Lesson 4) rather than a forced pick.
10Has a documented instance of changing a previously stated position after new evidence, and can describe what specifically changed their mind.

5. Decision quality

1Decisions are made and acted on with no written reasoning to review later.
4Occasionally uses the decision journal template, incompletely.
7Uses the full decision journal (objective, reversibility, worst/best/likely case) on decisions that actually matter, with review dates that get kept.
10Has at least one reviewed decision where the review distinguished "good reasoning, bad outcome" or "bad reasoning, lucky outcome" from "good reasoning, good outcome" — evidence the review checks process, not just results.

6. Self-examination and consistency

1No pattern-level self-review; every incident is evaluated in isolation.
4Notices a repeated pattern when someone else points it out.
7Weekly reviews (Lesson 20) consistently identify one real recurring pattern, not a different one every week by coincidence.
10Has used the "repeated for years" question (Lesson 19) to change a real decision, with the change documented.

How to score yourself honestly

Score each dimension from evidence you can point to — a specific journal entry, a specific remembered moment — not from a general sense of having improved. If you can't point to a specific instance for a level, you haven't reached it yet, regardless of how the last few weeks have felt.

Reassess monthly, alongside the behavioral audit.
