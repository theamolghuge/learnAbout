The Five-Limb Method

Pañcāvayava, applied to any claim, belief, or decision. From Lesson 5 — full explanation and worked example there.

10-second version

Claim. Ground. Break it. Fit. Verdict.

Full checklist

LimbDo thisChecks to run
1. Pratijna
claim		 State precisely what you're concluding. Do I actually have a hetu, or is this unexamined?
2. Hetu
reason		 State your reason. Which pramana is this — perception, inference, comparison, or testimony? If testimony: does the source actually know it, mean to be honest, and communicate clearly? (apta test)
3. Udaharana
rule + example		 State the general rule out loud. Hunt for a counter-example. Vyapti: is there a real case where the sign is present but the conclusion is false? Hetvabhasa: is the sign inconstant (savyabhicara)? Does it actually argue the opposite (viruddha)? Is there an equally strong opposing reason (satpratipaksha)?
4. Upanaya
application		 Confirm the specific case fits the rule. Is something directly observable already contradicting this (badhita)? Direct perception overrides inference.
5. Nigamana
conclusion		 Accept, reject, or suspend. If any earlier limb broke: suspend, and name what evidence would resolve it. Don't force a verdict.

When to use it

  • Before acting on a strong reaction to something someone said or did
  • Before accepting a claim from news, social media, or a confident-sounding source
  • Before treating a hunch about someone's motive as settled fact
  • Any time you notice yourself already certain, unusually fast

This card will grow specialized variants (for emotional situations, decisions, conflicts, information evaluation, self-examination) as later lessons reach those parts of the course — see Lesson 5 and NOTES.md for the plan.
