# Mission: Anvikshiki — Examined Life, Applied Daily

## Why
No single triggering event — this is a deliberate, long-term practice of disciplined inquiry (Anvikshiki, the Indian tradition of examination and reasoned judgment), taken on so that reasoning, decision-making, and self-observation become a default habit rather than something reached for only in a crisis or an argument.

## Success looks like
- Pauses and asks "is this a fact or my interpretation?" before reacting to something someone said or did
- Can name the hidden assumption(s) inside a belief or decision before acting on it
- Distinguishes what they actually know from what they're inferring, and says "I don't know" instead of guessing
- Revises a stated belief when shown contradicting evidence, without treating it as a personal defeat
- Runs a real decision (career, money, relationship) through a written framework instead of gut-reacting to it
- Notices and names a strong emotional reaction and inserts a deliberate reasoning step before acting on the impulse
- Keeps a running decision/belief journal and revisits it to see, concretely, where past reasoning went wrong

## Constraints
- ~30 minutes/day, no prior background in logic, philosophy, or Indian intellectual traditions — every technical or Sanskrit term must be defined in plain language on first use
- This is a long-running, multi-session curriculum, not a single document — sequencing and pacing are the teacher's call (see NOTES.md for the current plan)

## Out of scope (for now)
- Deep Sanskrit grammar or philology
- A comprehensive survey of all six classical darshanas (Vaisheshika, Mimamsa, Vedanta, etc.) beyond what's needed to place Anvikshiki and Nyaya in context
- Comparative religion or theological debate
- Joining a real-world discussion community — worth proposing once there's enough grounding to make it useful, but not assumed yet; ask first

## The arc
This mirrors the classical progression from inquiry, to reasoning, to self-examination, to decision, to conduct — taught as sequential clusters rather than dumped all at once:
1. **Foundations** — what Anvikshiki is, its core vocabulary, why it existed
2. **The reasoning toolkit** — observation, fact vs. interpretation, assumptions, evidence, inference, causation, uncertainty (grounded in the classical *pramana*, "means of knowledge," framework)
3. **Reasoning errors** — classical *hetvabhasa* (fallacies of reason) alongside modern cognitive biases, made behavioral, not academic
4. **A repeatable method of inquiry** — built from the actual structure of classical Indian reasoning, for problems, beliefs, and decisions
5. **Turning inward** — emotions, self-inquiry, what actions reveal about real priorities
6. **Applying it outward** — decisions, relationships, speech, evaluating information, ethics
7. **Making it a habit** — daily/weekly/monthly practice, journals, a personal operating system

Clusters 1–4 are foundational to everything after. Expect the first several sessions to stay there before branching into decisions, relationships, and habit systems — teaching the method before the vocabulary is built on sand.
