Negotiation Toolkit

Grows one technique at a time, each with its own lesson. Based on Chris Voss's Never Split the Difference — full citation in RESOURCES.md.

Calibrated Questions

Open-ended questions starting with What or How — never "Why" (accusatory) and never a yes/no closed question (easy to refuse). They hand your problem back to the other side to solve, while giving them the feeling of control. See Lesson 11.

  • "How am I supposed to do that?" — Voss's own favorite, delivered as a genuine ask for help, not sarcasm.
  • "What about this is important to you?"
  • "How does this affect the rest of your team?"
  • "What's the biggest challenge you're facing here?"

Mirroring

Repeat the last 1–3 words the other person said, as a question. Zero opinion of your own attached; reliably makes people keep talking. See Lesson 13.

Labeling

Name an emotion you observe in someone, without claiming to feel it yourself: "It seems like…" / "It sounds like…". Defuses a named negative emotion; amplifies a named positive one. See Lesson 13.

The Three Kinds of Yes

Counterfeit (an easier exit than saying no, no real commitment), Confirmation (an innocent reflex to a black-or-white question), and Commitment (the real deal, backed by real reasons). Test which one you're getting with the Rule of Three: get it reaffirmed three separate times, through different calibrated questions or labels — hard to fake three times over. See Lesson 19.

Stated vs. Real Objection

The objection someone states first is often not the real one. Label it, then ask a calibrated question to surface what's actually underneath, and address that instead. See Lesson 19.
