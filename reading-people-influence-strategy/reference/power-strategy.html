Grows one concept per lesson. Grounded in Thomas Schelling's The Strategy of Conflict and Robert Greene's 48 Laws of Power — full citations in RESOURCES.md.

Credible Commitment

Schelling's paradox: voluntarily and visibly removing your own ability to back down makes your position stronger, not weaker — because your counterpart now has to treat your stated position as fixed. Only works if the commitment carries a real, third-party-verifiable cost to breaking it. See Lesson 14.

Greene's Lens: Never Outshine the Master

Read defensively, never as personal strategy: people in authority who feel insecure in their position sometimes turn against whoever makes them look or feel inferior — even someone acting in total good faith. A repeated (not one-off) cooling toward you specifically after visible wins is the signal worth acting on. See Lesson 17.
