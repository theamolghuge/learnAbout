Reading Nonverbal Behavior

This grows as later lessons add specific signals (voice, posture, micro-expressions). The method below is the load-bearing piece — everything else is detail on top of it.

The method: Baseline → Deviation → Cluster → Context

  1. Baseline — how does this specific person normally act, in a low-stakes moment? (Not a universal norm — their own resting state.)
  2. Deviation — when a particular topic or question comes up, does their behavior change from their own baseline?
  3. Cluster — did more than one channel shift at once (voice, posture, gesture, pacing)? A single change means much less than several changes landing together.
  4. Context — what else could explain it, before you reach for "they're hiding something"? (Cold room, uncomfortable chair, unrelated stress, a stiff shoulder.)

A deviation cluster is a flag to ask a question, not a verdict. See Lesson 4 for the full walkthrough, and a future negotiation lesson on calibrated questions for what to actually do with the flag once you notice it.

Timing: the missing variable

The same ostentatious display can mean opposite things depending on when it happens relative to the stakes. Early, before much is at risk, it tends to correlate with weakness (cheap theater). Later, right after a genuinely costly move, the same display tends to correlate with strength (bluffers under-invest in performance exactly when getting caught is expensive). This is Zachary Elwood's correction to the popular but oversimplified "weak means strong" poker myth — see Lesson 15.

What this is not

There is no reliable dictionary where a single gesture maps to a single meaning ("crossed arms = defensive," "no eye contact = lying") — these claims are widely repeated and do not hold up as universal rules. Even microexpressions, the most studied nonverbal signal, occur in only about 2% of real expressions and appear at similar rates whether someone is lying or telling the truth (Porter & ten Brinke). Treat any single-cue claim, including ones in this workspace, skeptically.
