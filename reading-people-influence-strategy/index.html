  7. 07 Authority Milgram's lab coat — trust in the costume of expertise, and separating it from the real thing. Persuasion
  8. 08 Liking The Tupperware party effect, the halo effect, and asking what's left of an argument once you strip the liking away. Persuasion

  10. 10 Unity Cialdini's 7th principle — shared identity, and affinity fraud as its most damaging form. Completes the Cialdini series. Persuasion
  11. 11 Calibrated Questions Voss's "How am I supposed to do that?" — open questions that hand a problem back without revealing your position. Negotiation
  12. 12 Dark Triad: Spotting the Pattern Narcissism, Machiavellianism, psychopathy — and the empathy nuance that matters most. Pattern-recognition, not diagnosis. Manipulation Awareness
  13. 13 Mirroring & Labeling The waitress tipping study, and getting more from someone while revealing nothing of your own. Negotiation
  14. 14 Credible Commitments Schelling's paradox — burning your own bridges to make your position stronger, and testing whether someone else's is real. Power & Strategy
  15. 15 Reading a Bluff: Poker Tells in Practice Elwood's correction to the "weak means strong" myth — timing, not the gesture alone, is what actually predicts strength. Nonverbal Reading
  16. 16 Social Engineering: Pretexting & Elicitation The FBI's own elicitation-awareness brochure — every technique is a Cialdini principle aimed at information, not a sale. Manipulation Awareness
  17. 17 Greene's Lens: Reading Power Plays "Never Outshine the Master" — recognizing when a superior's insecurity, not your performance, is driving how they treat you. Power & Strategy
  18. 18 The Cold Reading Skeleton Forer's 1948 study — telling genuine active listening from a Barnum statement that only feels personal. Active Listening & Cold Reading
  19. 19 Reframing "No" Voss's three kinds of yes, and surfacing the real objection behind the one someone actually states. Sales & Objection-Handling
  20. 20 Negative Visualization Marcus Aurelius's morning rehearsal — pre-loading your reaction so difficult people can't catch you by surprise. Self-Regulation
  21. 21 Thinking in Forcing Moves Chess's "forcing moves first" habit, synthesizing calibrated questions, credible commitments, and tell-reading into one idea. Strategic Thinking
