# Mission: Reading People, Resisting Manipulation, and Strategic Influence

## Why
No single triggering event — this is a standing self-improvement project. The underlying outcome: in any interaction (negotiating a deal, sitting across from a difficult boss or relative, sensing when someone's playing an angle, or just talking to a stranger), you want to accurately read what's really going on, keep your own composure and tells under control, and choose your move deliberately instead of reacting on impulse. The point isn't trivia about Cialdini or Voss — it's that six months from now, you notice manipulation as it's happening, stay level under pressure, and can steer a conversation toward an outcome you actually want.

## Success looks like
- You can name which influence tactic (reciprocity, social proof, scarcity, etc.) is being used on you, in the moment, before you comply with it
- You can read a stranger's baseline body language and spot a meaningful deviation from it (not just recite "crossed arms = defensive")
- You've run at least one real negotiation (salary, purchase, household dispute — anything with real stakes) using calibrated questions and mirroring, and can say what worked
- You catch your own reactive pattern (the flash of anger, the urge to over-explain, the need to fill silence) before it drives your behavior — and have a journal showing this happening more often over time
- You can hold silence and ambiguity under social pressure without needing to fill it
- You can articulate, for a real person in your life, what's driving their behavior strategically (their incentives, their likely next move) rather than just their stated words
- You know the difference between "influence" (ethical, mutual) and "manipulation" (exploitative, one-sided) and can name which one you're on the receiving end of

## Constraints
- Starting from zero on all of this — no prior reading of Cialdini, Voss, Greene, Ekman, or poker-tells material. Lessons need to build from first principles, not assume familiarity.
- Wants daily micro-practice: short (5–15 min) real-world observation/journaling drills, with lessons layered in periodically — not a once-a-week lecture format.
- Explicit emphasis on practical application over theory: every lesson should end in a real-world action (an observation to make, a question to try, a journal entry to write), not just reading.
- Wants to stay "unreadable and non-reactive" under pressure — emotional regulation and self-observation are treated as load-bearing, not an afterthought bolted onto the end.

## Out of scope (for now)
- Clinical-grade diagnosis of personality disorders (narcissism, psychopathy, etc.) — dark triad content is covered for *pattern recognition and self-protection*, not amateur diagnosis of real people in the user's life.
- Deep mathematical game theory (formal proofs, equilibrium computation) — Schelling and game theory are covered at the level of strategic intuition (commitment, credible threats, focal points), not textbook math.
- Actual social-engineering/hacking tradecraft (phishing infrastructure, physical pentesting) — covered only to the extent it teaches recognizing manipulation tactics used against the user, not to execute attacks.
- Competitive poker mastery (ranges, GTO solvers) — poker is used purely as a tell-reading and strategic-thinking training ground.
