# Reading People, Influence & Strategy — Resources

## Knowledge

### Persuasion & Influence
- [Book: _Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion_ — Robert Cialdini](https://www.amazon.com/Influence-Psychology-Persuasion-Robert-Cialdini/dp/006124189X)
  The foundational text on the six (originally) principles of persuasion: reciprocity, commitment/consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity (Cialdini later added a seventh, "unity"). Cialdini embedded himself with real "compliance professionals" to derive these. Use for: recognizing influence tactics in the moment, sales, and understanding what manipulation borrows from legitimate persuasion.

### Manipulation & Dark Triad Awareness
- [Paulhus & Williams (2002), "The Dark Triad of Personality: Narcissism, Machiavellianism, and Psychopathy"](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~dpaulhus/research/SELF-ENHANCEMENT/downloads/ARTICLES/JRP.02.pdf)
  The original peer-reviewed paper that coined the term. Use for: the actual empirical definitions, not the pop-psychology version — grounding for spotting patterns without amateur-diagnosing people.
- [Psychology Today: Dark Triad overview](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/dark-triad)
  Accessible, clinically-grounded summary. Use for: quick refreshers on trait definitions.
- [Book: _Social Engineering: The Science of Human Hacking_ — Christopher Hadnagy](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Social+Engineering:+The+Science+of+Human+Hacking,+2nd+Edition-p-9781119433750)
  Written by a professional social engineer; covers pretexting, elicitation, and influence-as-attack. Use for: recognizing when someone is running a deliberate information-gathering or trust-building play on you, not just reading it as innocent small talk.

### Power & Strategy
- [Book: _The 48 Laws of Power_ — Robert Greene](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/149049/the-48-laws-of-power-by-robert-greene/)
  Read as a descriptive field guide to how power actually gets used against you — a "know thine enemy" reference, not a personal code of conduct. Several laws are ethically ugly by design; the goal is pattern recognition, not adoption. Use for: naming a power play once you're already on the receiving end of one.
- [Book: _The Strategy of Conflict_ — Thomas C. Schelling](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674840317)
  The classic text on commitment, credible threats, and focal points — game theory applied to real bargaining and conflict, not textbook math. Use for: understanding why "burning your own bridges" or making a threat public can be a rational move.
  [Companion: Roger Myerson, "Learning from Schelling's Strategy of Conflict" (PDF)](https://home.uchicago.edu/~rmyerson/stratofc2010.pdf) — a readable modern gloss on the book's core ideas.

### Negotiation
- [Book: _Never Split the Difference_ — Chris Voss](https://www.blackswanltd.com/never-split-the-difference)
  Ex-FBI hostage negotiator's field manual: calibrated questions, tactical empathy, mirroring, labeling. Use for: the core negotiation toolkit for salary talks, purchases, disputes.
- [PON — Program on Negotiation at Harvard Law School](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/)
  Free, continuously-updated, academically rigorous negotiation blog. Use for: grounding Voss's tactics in the wider academic negotiation literature, and for real scenario walkthroughs.

### Body Language & Nonverbal Reading
- [Paul Ekman Group — Micro Expressions](https://www.paulekman.com/resources/micro-expressions/)
  Ekman co-discovered microexpressions and built the field's core training tool (METT). Use for: the science of universal facial emotion signals — and its real limits (microexpressions are rare; training improves *recognition*, not lie detection).
  Caveat worth keeping in view: [Porter & ten Brinke's research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8406528/) found true microexpressions occur in only ~2% of expressions, and appear at similar rates whether someone is lying or not — read microexpression claims skeptically, including this workspace's own lessons on it.
- [The Behavior Panel (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/c/thebehaviorpanel) — panel of body-language professionals (Mark Bowden, Chase Hughes, Scott Rouse, Greg Hartley) breaking down real footage. Use for: worked examples of baseline-and-deviation reading, with the channel's own disclaimer taken seriously (opinion, not forensic fact about any real person).
- [Science of People — Vanessa Van Edwards](https://www.scienceofpeople.com/microexpressions/)
  Practical, well-organized synthesis aimed at non-experts. Use for: a gentler on-ramp before the denser Ekman material.

### Poker & Chess-Style Strategic Thinking
- [Book: _Reading Poker Tells_ — Zachary Elwood](https://readingpokertells.video/)
  Widely regarded as the gold-standard text on poker tells; former pro player, tells consultant for WSOP final tablists. Use for: the general *framework* (baseline behavior, situational context, tell clusters) that transfers to reading anyone, not just poker opponents.
- [Dan Heisman on forcing moves (checks, captures, threats)](https://www.danheisman.com/definitions.html)
  National Master and longtime chess instructor; his "forcing moves first" calculation habit is standard, widely-taught chess pedagogy. Use for: the general skill of narrowing an opponent's realistic options before calculating further — transfers directly to negotiation and reading people.

### Emotional Regulation & Stoicism
- [Epictetus, _Enchiridion_ (public domain, trans. Elizabeth Carter)](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html)
  The primary source for the dichotomy of control — the root idea this whole workspace's self-regulation practice is built on. Free, short, and the actual original text rather than a summary of it.
- [Book: _A Guide to the Good Life: The Ancient Art of Stoic Joy_ — William B. Irvine](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/a-guide-to-the-good-life-9780195374612)
  Turns scattered Stoic writing into a systematic modern practice: negative visualization, the dichotomy of control, the "reserve clause." Use for: the practical exercises this workspace's journaling drills are adapted from.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [r/socialskills](https://www.reddit.com/r/socialskills/) (6M+ members)
  Large, active community for real-world social-skills troubleshooting. Use for: posting a specific interaction you're stuck on and getting outside read.
- [r/Stoicism](https://www.reddit.com/r/Stoicism/)
  Active, well-moderated community discussing practical application of Stoic philosophy. Use for: checking your interpretation of a Stoic principle against people who've read the primary texts.
- [r/negotiation](https://www.reddit.com/r/negotiation/)
  Smaller but relevant community for negotiation-specific war stories and critique.
- Local: a home poker game, low-stakes casino table, or a **Toastmasters** chapter
  Use for: live-fire practice reading people and staying composed under real (if low) social stakes — no online community substitutes for being in the room.
