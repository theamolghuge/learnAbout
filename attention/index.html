Teaching workspace

Attention Management

Going deep on one thing for hours, in a world engineered to fragment focus — the skill, from first principles to daily practice.

Mission: get your attention back from too many competing pursuits, so a few of them can become great instead of all of them staying mediocre. See MISSION.md. Everything below is self-contained: you don't need to read any external book to complete a lesson, though primary sources are linked throughout for depth and trust.

Lessons

Work through these in order — each builds on the last.

Lesson 1 · ~8 min
Why Your Attention Fragments
Attention residue, the 23-minute recovery cost of interruption, and why 90% of distraction starts inside you, not on your phone. Ends with a 10-minute single-tasking practice.
Lesson 2 · ~9 min
Designing Your Environment
Fixing external triggers with friction (effort/price/identity pacts) and internal triggers with a rehearsed if-then plan. Ends with you writing your own implementation intention.
Lesson 3 · ~9 min
Time Blocking and the Shutdown Ritual
Turning one good session into a repeatable daily system: scheduling deep work in defended blocks, and a fixed end-of-day ritual so work stops bleeding into the rest of your life. Ends with a live time-block builder and your own shutdown phrase.
Lesson 4 · ~10 min · Closest to your mission
Choosing Your Few Things
Why spreading effort across many pursuits costs more than it looks like (goal-conflict research, career capital theory, essentialism), and a forced-ranking exercise to surface your actual vital few and an explicit avoid-at-all-costs list.
Lesson 5 · ~10 min · Sustaining the session
Recovery and Flow
Why directed attention depletes and a scrolling break doesn't restore it (Attention Restoration Theory, soft fascination), plus calibrating challenge vs. skill so focus blocks self-sustain instead of running on willpower.
Next lesson Not built yet: protecting the vital few day to day — saying no to new opportunities, auditing existing commitments against your avoid-at-all-costs list, and mechanisms beyond willpower. Best once you've actually completed Lesson 4's ranking exercise. Just ask when you're ready.
