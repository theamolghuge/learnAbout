# Attention Management Resources

## Knowledge

- [Book: _Deep Work: Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World_ — Cal Newport](https://www.calnewport.com/books/deep-work/)
  The foundational practitioner's text. Defines deep work vs. shallow work and gives the four operating rules (work deeply, embrace boredom, quit social media, drain the shallows). Use for: building a personal deep-work system, routines/rituals, the case against busyness-as-proxy-for-productivity.
- [Book: _Digital Minimalism_ — Cal Newport](https://www.calnewport.com/books/digital-minimalism/)
  Companion to Deep Work, focused specifically on redesigning your relationship with attention-grabbing technology. Use for: environment/tool design, the 30-day digital declutter method.
- [Book: _Attention Span: Find Focus, Fight Distraction_ — Gloria Mark, PhD](https://gloriamark.com/attention-span/)
  Two decades of empirical research (UC Irvine) on how attention actually behaves under modern digital conditions — not folklore. Use for: any factual claim about attention-span length or interruption-recovery time.
- [Paper: "Why Is It So Hard to Do My Work? The Challenge of Attention Residue When Switching Between Tasks" — Sophie Leroy (2009), *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v109y2009i2p168-181.html)
  The original experimental paper defining "attention residue." Use for: explaining why task-switching quietly degrades performance even without visible distraction, and why finishing (or consciously closing) a task matters before switching.
- [Wikipedia: Attention Restoration Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attention_restoration_theory) + [Systematic review — *Journal of Toxicology and Environmental Health*](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10937404.2016.1196155)
  Rachel & Stephen Kaplan's theory that directed attention is a finite, depletable resource restored by exposure to "soft fascination" (nature). Use for: designing recovery periods between deep-work blocks, not just the blocks themselves.
- [Overview: Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi and Flow Theory — Positive Psychology](https://positivepsychology.com/mihaly-csikszentmihalyi-father-of-flow/)
  Origin of "flow": the challenge-skill balance and the other 8 antecedents of total absorption. Use for: calibrating task difficulty so deep work sessions self-sustain rather than requiring constant willpower.
- [Book: _Indistractable: How to Control Your Attention and Choose Your Life_ — Nir Eyal](https://www.nirandfar.com/indistractable/)
  A behavioral-design framework: master internal triggers → make time for traction → hack back external triggers → prevent distraction with pacts. Use for: understanding that ~90% of distraction originates from internal discomfort (boredom, anxiety), not the notification itself.
- [Paper: "Implementation Intentions" — Peter Gollwitzer (1999)](https://www.prospectivepsych.org/sites/default/files/pictures/Gollwitzer_Implementation-intentions-1999.pdf)
  The original if-then planning research. Use for: turning "I'll focus better" into a specific, automatic, situational trigger-response rule.
- [Review: "The impact of meditation on sustained attention" — PMC, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11925505/)
  Meta-review of controlled studies on meditation and attention. Use for: evidence that attention span is trainable (not fixed), and realistic dosage (brief daily practice shows measurable effect).
- [The Time-Block Planner — Cal Newport](https://www.timeblockplanner.com/)
  The operational tool version of Deep Work's scheduling advice. Use for: the mechanics of daily time-block planning and the "shutdown complete" ritual.
- [Book: _So Good They Can't Ignore You_ — Cal Newport](https://www.calnewport.com/books/so-good-they-cant-ignore-you/)
  Career capital theory: rare, valuable skills come from deliberate practice concentrated in one area over time, not from spreading effort across many. Use for: the case for depth over breadth at the project/career level, not just the single-session level.
  Also relevant: [_So Good They Can't Ignore You_ summary — Commoncog](https://commoncog.com/so-good-they-cant-ignore-you/) as a fast overview.
- [Book: _Essentialism: The Disciplined Pursuit of Less_ — Greg McKeown](https://gregmckeown.com/books/essentialism/)
  "The disciplined pursuit of less but better" — a framework for deliberately choosing a small set of priorities and saying a genuine no to everything else. Use for: the philosophy behind narrowing focus, and language for explaining that choice to other people.
- [Meta-analysis: "Goal conflict and psychological well-being" — ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0092656616302781)
  Peer-reviewed evidence that pursuing multiple goals which compete for the same finite resources (time, attention) measurably reduces progress on all of them and lowers well-being. Use for: the research-backed (not just anecdotal) case that "a few things done well" beats "many things done partially."

## Knowledge — used with a caveat
- The "Warren Buffett 25/5 rule" (write 25 goals, circle your top 5, avoid the other 20 at all costs) is a popular prioritization exercise, but the origin story (a conversation with Buffett's pilot) has been [debunked as likely fabricated](https://www.inc.com/marcel-schwantes/warren-buffett-25-5-rule-career-goals.html) — no verified source ties it to Buffett. The exercise itself is used in Lesson 4 anyway because the underlying logic (forced ranking + an explicit "avoid" list) is sound and testable on its own merits — but it's presented to the user as "a popular exercise with a disputed origin," not as something Buffett actually said.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [r/getdisciplined](https://reddit.com/r/getdisciplined)
  ~2.2M members, the largest general self-discipline/productivity hub. Use for: troubleshooting a stalled habit, seeing how others structure deep-work routines, reality-checking a plan against people who've tried it.
- [r/productivity](https://reddit.com/r/productivity)
  Adjacent, more tool/system-focused. Use for: workflow and environment-design ideas — treat tool recommendations skeptically, they trend toward novelty.
- [Focusmate](https://www.focusmate.com/)
  Virtual "body doubling" co-working — you book a session, show up on video with a stranger, both state intentions, both work in silence. Directly operationalizes several of Newport's/Eyal's ideas (external accountability, timeboxed sessions). Use for: days when starting is the hard part, not sustaining.
