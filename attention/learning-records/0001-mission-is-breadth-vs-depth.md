# Mission is fundamentally about breadth vs. depth, not just session-level distraction

The user's stated goal (2026-08-05): "get back my attention to focus on things that matter instead of diverting my focus on multiple things, so as to achieve greatness in few things instead of jumping around and not doing any." This reframes the mission beyond single-session focus (Lessons 1–3, which teach how to sustain attention *within* a session) to add a life/project-level layer: deliberately narrowing *what* gets pursued at all, so depth compounds instead of being divided across too many things.

**Why it matters for future sessions:** teaching should now cover two related but distinct skills — (a) sustaining focus once you've chosen what to work on, and (b) choosing the small set of things worth that focus and saying no to the rest. Don't conflate them; a lesson that only teaches (a) will feel incomplete against this mission. Lesson 4 ("Choosing Your Few Things") was built specifically to address (b), via career-capital theory and a structured prioritization exercise (Buffett's 25/5 method), rather than by asking the user directly what their "few things" are — they didn't answer a similar direct question earlier in this same session, so a structured exercise was used instead of a repeat interrogation.
