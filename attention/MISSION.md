# Mission: Attention Management

## Why
You want to stop diverting your focus across many things and instead concentrate it on a few things — the belief being that jumping between pursuits produces mediocrity everywhere, while sustained focus on a small number of things is what actually produces greatness. This mission has two layers, and both matter: (1) the **session-level** skill of sustained, undistracted attention (covered in Lessons 1–3), and (2) the **life/project-level** skill of choosing *which few things* deserve that attention and deliberately excluding the rest — which is the layer this update is about.

**Status: confirmed at this level of detail (2026-08-05).** Still open: what the candidate "few things" actually are for you right now (a skill, a career, a creative practice, a business — one thing or a small handful?). That's deliberately the subject of Lesson 4's exercise rather than a chat question, since you're better served by a structured way to surface the answer than by being asked directly again.

## Success looks like
- You can name your current "vital few" — a short, explicit list — and can also name what you've deliberately said no to in order to protect them.
- You can sit down and do 60-90 minutes of cognitively demanding work with zero task-switches, on demand, not just on lucky days (Lessons 1–3).
- You recognize the specific moment attention starts to fracture (an internal urge, a completed sub-task, a notification) and have a rehearsed response instead of following it automatically.
- You can explain, from evidence rather than folklore, why spreading effort across many pursuits costs more than it looks like it does — both within a single session (attention residue, switch cost) and across a life (career capital / compounding depth) — and you use that to make different decisions about what you take on.
- When a new opportunity or project shows up, you have a real mechanism (not just willpower) for deciding whether it's one of the few things or a distraction from them.
