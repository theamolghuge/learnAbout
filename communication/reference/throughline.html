The Throughline

One idea, 15 words or fewer. From Lesson 5.

The rule

Before you write a single slide or line of a talk, write the one idea it exists to deliver, in 15 words or fewer. Every part of the talk should hang off this sentence — if a story, a stat, or a slide doesn't support the throughline, cut it.

Real examples

"Vulnerability is something to be treasured, not hidden from." (Brené Brown)
"More choice actually makes us less happy." (Barry Schwartz)
"Education's potential is transformed if you focus on the creativity of kids." (Ken Robinson)

How it relates to what you already know

The throughline is the answer from the Pyramid Principle, one level up — it's the "answer" of the entire talk, not just one response. Once you have it, your talk's main sections should be its MECE supporting reasons.

Quick test

Quick test Say your throughline to someone with no context. If they can roughly guess what your talk's three main sections would be, it's specific enough. If they say "okay, but about what, exactly?" — it's still a topic, not an idea.
