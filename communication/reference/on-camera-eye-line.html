On-Camera Eye Line

Talk to the lens, not the screen. From Lesson 6.

The core fix

Look at the camera lens itself, not your own video preview, not the other person's face on screen. The lens is the only point in the room that transmits eye contact to the viewer.

Quick test If you can see your own face while you talk, you're probably watching yourself instead of the lens. Cover or shrink your self-preview if your software allows it.

The one-person trick

Don't picture "an audience." Picture one specific real person — a particular friend or colleague — and talk to them. This single mental substitution tends to fix tone, pacing, and warmth simultaneously, because you already know how to talk to one person; you don't know how to talk to "everyone."

Energy calibration: live vs. recorded

  • Live (video call, livestream): normal conversational energy — the same as talking to someone across a table.
  • Recorded solo (no one reacting back): push energy up slightly, since you don't have a real-time reaction feeding your pacing and tone the way a live listener does.
