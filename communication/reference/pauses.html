The Three Pauses

Silence, used on purpose. From Lesson 4.

The three types

  • Emphatic pause — right before or after a key word/phrase, to make it stand out. Use on your answer sentence.
  • Cognitive pause — after complex information, giving the listener a moment to process before you move to the next point. Use between your supporting reasons.
  • Dramatic pause — a longer silence before or after a big statement, to build tension or let it land. Use sparingly — once per talk, not once per sentence.

Where to place them in an answer-first structure

[Answer sentence] — emphatic pause —
"There are three reasons." — cognitive pause —
"First, …" — cognitive pause —
"Second, …" — cognitive pause —
"Third, …"

The most common mistake

Filling silence with "um," "so," or "you know" instead of letting it be silent. A pause reads as confidence; a filler word reads as discomfort with silence. If you feel the urge to fill it, that's the signal you're mid-pause — let it finish instead.

Quick test Count "one-one-thousand, two-one-thousand" silently in your head after your answer sentence. It will feel far too long to you and completely normal to the listener — nerves compress your sense of time.
