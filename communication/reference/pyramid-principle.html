The Pyramid Principle

Answer-first structure, in one page. From Lesson 1.

The shape

Answer (your one-sentence point / recommendation)
  ├─ Supporting reason 1 (+ evidence)
  ├─ Supporting reason 2 (+ evidence)
  └─ Supporting reason 3 (+ evidence)

Say the answer first. Everything after it exists only to support that one sentence.

MECE — how to group your reasons

Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive. Your supporting points should not overlap, and together they should cover the whole case — no gaps, no repeats.

Quick test If two of your reasons could be merged into one without losing meaning, they weren't mutually exclusive. If someone could ask "but what about …?" and you have no bucket for it, you weren't collectively exhaustive.

SCQA — for when you need to set up the answer

Useful when the answer alone would be jarring without context (e.g. delivering bad news, or answering a question nobody asked yet):

  • Situation — the stable, agreed-upon starting point
  • Complication — what changed / the problem
  • Question — the question this raises (often implicit)
  • Answer — your point, then the pyramid of support

The on-the-spot template

Say this, in order "[Answer, one sentence]. There are three reasons for that: first, …; second, …; third, …."

Common failure modes

  • Bottom-up narrative — walking through the story in the order you discovered it, arriving at the point last. (The default failure mode under nerves.)
  • Answer buried in a subordinate clause — "So, given all that, I guess what I'd say is …" three sentences in.
  • Non-MECE reasons — three reasons that are really one reason said three ways.
