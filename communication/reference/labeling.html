Labeling

Naming an emotion, tentatively, before responding to content. From Lesson 3.

The starters

"It sounds like …"
"It seems like …"
"It looks like …"

Always tentative, never declarative. Not "you're angry" — "it sounds like you're frustrated." The hedge leaves room for them to correct you, which itself builds trust.

The two rules

  • Statement, not question. "It sounds like you're worried about the timeline" — not "Are you worried about the timeline?" A question asks them to do the emotional labor of confirming; a statement does the labor for them and just needs a nod or a correction.
  • Then stop talking. The label creates a silence. Let it sit for a beat or two — don't rush into your response. The silence is what gets the real information out.

Common failure modes

  • Jumping to solutions before labeling — "well, we can just push the deadline" — skips past the fact that they needed to be heard first.
  • Over-confident labels — naming the wrong emotion so firmly it feels like you're arguing with them about how they feel. If you're wrong, they'll correct you — that's fine, that's the point.
  • Filling the pause — talking through the silence right after the label, which erases its effect.
