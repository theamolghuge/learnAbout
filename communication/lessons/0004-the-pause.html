The Pause

Silence is a tool, not a gap to be filled. Three specific pauses, and where to put them in the structure you already know.

You now have a structure — answer, then reasons (Lesson 1) — and you know how to write it (Lesson 2) and how to make room for someone else's feelings in conversation (Lesson 3). This lesson is about delivery: the same structure said with silence used on purpose sounds confident. Said with every gap filled by "um" or rushed through, it sounds nervous — even if the words are identical.

Three pauses, three jobs

  • Emphatic — right after your answer sentence, to let it land before you move to support.
  • Cognitive — between each supporting reason, giving the listener a beat to absorb one before the next arrives.
  • Dramatic — a longer silence around one big statement in a whole talk. Rare, deliberate, not a habit.
No pauses: "We're about two weeks behind schedule um there's three reasons so like the vendor was slow at first and then uh there was a scoping issue in June and also we were kind of understaffed over summer."
With pauses: "We're about two weeks behind schedule." (pause) "Three reasons." (pause) "A slow vendor start." (pause) "A scoping issue in June." (pause) "And being understaffed through summer."

Read both out loud. The second isn't just clearer — it sounds like someone who is sure of what they're saying. Full reference: Reference: The Three Pauses.

Drill (do this now — ~10 minutes, recording required)

Re-deliver your Lesson 1 answer, on purpose

  1. Pull up the answer-first line you built in Lesson 1's drill (or make a new one if you didn't save it — any answer + 3 reasons works).
  2. Record yourself saying it at your normal pace, no deliberate pauses.
  3. Record it again, this time counting silently to two after the answer sentence (emphatic), and to one between each reason (cognitive). Resist the urge to say "um" in the gap — just let it be silent.
  4. Listen to both back to back.

Check your work: the paused version should sound more deliberate and confident, even though it's objectively slower. If it sounds like you're just talking slowly rather than pausing, the difference is: pace stays normal, pauses are silent gaps, not stretched-out words.

Retrieval check

Which pause type goes right after your answer sentence?

The emphatic pause.

What's the most common way people fill a pause instead of letting it be silent?

Filler words like "um," "so," or "you know."
