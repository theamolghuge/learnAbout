The Throughline

Before you write a single slide, compress the whole talk into one sentence. This is the Pyramid Principle applied to an entire talk instead of one answer.

Chris Anderson, who has run TED since the early 2000s, watched thousands of talks get built and noticed the good ones all had one thing in common: a single idea the whole talk existed to deliver, expressible in one sentence. He calls it the throughline — a rope every part of the talk hangs from.

The rule: 15 words or fewer

"Vulnerability is something to be treasured, not hidden from." — Brené Brown
"More choice actually makes us less happy." — Barry Schwartz

Notice these aren't topics ("my talk is about vulnerability"). They're claims — specific enough that you could disagree with them. That specificity is what makes a talk have a spine instead of just being a tour of a subject.

Why this is Lesson 1, one level up

In Lesson 1 you learned: say the answer first, then 2–3 MECE reasons. A throughline is exactly that "answer" — just for a 5-minute update or a full talk instead of one spoken response. Once you have the throughline, your talk's sections should be its supporting reasons.

Drill (do this now — ~12 minutes)

Compress a talk you might actually give

  1. Pick a real topic you could plausibly be asked to speak on — a work project, a hobby, an opinion you hold strongly. Not hypothetical: something you'd actually say yes to.
  2. Write three throughline attempts, each 15 words or fewer, each a claim (not a topic label).
  3. Say each one out loud to an empty room (or record it). Notice which one you can say with a straight face and full conviction — that's usually the real one.
  4. Take your best throughline and, using Lesson 1's skill, write 3 MECE supporting points it would break into if you actually gave this talk.

Check your work: read only your throughline to someone (or read it back to yourself cold, an hour later). If it tells you roughly what the three sections would be about, it passed. If it still sounds like a topic ("about productivity"), tighten it into an actual claim ("most productivity advice fixes the wrong bottleneck").

Retrieval check

What's the maximum length of a throughline?

Fifteen words.

What's the difference between a throughline and a topic?

A throughline is a specific claim you could disagree with.
