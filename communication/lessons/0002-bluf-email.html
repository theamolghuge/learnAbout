BLUF: Answer-First Writing

Same skill as Lesson 1, new terrain. In Lesson 1 you learned to say your answer first. Now you'll write it first.

The military calls this BLUF — Bottom Line Up Front. The U.S. Army formalized it in 1988 because officers were drowning in memos where the actual point showed up in the last paragraph. It's the exact same move as the Pyramid Principle from Lesson 1, applied to something you write instead of say: a reader with 10 seconds should get your answer before they get anything else.

The shape

Buried (the default): "Hi team, hope you had a good weekend. Wanted to give a quick update on the vendor situation — we've been going back and forth with them since March, and there were some scoping issues in June that slowed things down, plus we were short-staffed over summer. Anyway, long story short, we're currently about two weeks behind on the migration."
BLUF: "Migration is ~2 weeks behind schedule. No action needed from you — flagging for visibility. Cause: slow vendor start, a June scoping issue, and summer understaffing. Back on pace since August; details below if useful."

Notice the second version answers three questions in the first two lines that the first version makes you dig for: what happened, does it affect me, do I need to do anything.

The two new pieces writing adds

Speaking BLUF (Lesson 1) only needed the answer + reasons. Written BLUF adds two things speech doesn't need, because the reader can't ask a clarifying question in real time:

  • An explicit action line — what the reader needs to do, and by when (or "no action needed").
  • A subject line that is itself an answer — not a topic like "Project Update," but the actual bottom line: "Migration ~2 weeks behind, no action needed."

Drill (do this now — ~12 minutes)

Rewrite a real email

  1. Find a real email — one you're about to send, or one you sent recently that felt clunky. (No fabricated examples — this only works on real stakes.)
  2. Circle the actual bottom line in the existing draft: the one sentence that is the real point.
  3. Write a new subject line that states that bottom line, not just the topic.
  4. Rewrite the first two lines: line 1 is the bottom line, line 2 is the action needed (or "no action needed").
  5. Move everything else below, shortest/most important reason first, background last. Delete any sentence that exists only to be polite ("Hope you're well," "Just wanted to follow up") — cut it entirely; the fact that you wrote already signals the courtesy.

Check your work: cover everything after line 2 with your hand. If a reader stopping there still knows what happened and what to do, you passed the two-line test. Count how many words you cut — that's a real measure of how much throat-clearing was in the original.

Retrieval check

What does BLUF stand for?

Bottom line up front.

What two elements does written BLUF need that spoken answer-first doesn't?

An explicit action line, and an answer-bearing subject line.
