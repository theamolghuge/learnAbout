# Communication Resources

Grounds every lesson in [MISSION.md](./MISSION.md). Organized by the seven tracks, then Wisdom (communities).

## Knowledge

### 1. Clarity of thought & articulation

- [The Pyramid Principle — Barbara Minto (summary + primary structure)](https://managementconsulted.com/pyramid-principle/)
  The original McKinsey "answer-first" framework: lead with the conclusion, group supporting arguments (MECE), back with evidence. Use for: structuring any argument, email, or spoken answer before delivering it.
- [The Feynman Technique — explained](https://collegeinfogeek.com/feynman-technique/)
  Four-step method (pick a concept → explain it to a beginner → find the gaps → simplify/re-explain) for compressing understanding into plain language. Use for: testing whether you actually understand something before trying to explain it to others.
- [Write Simply — Paul Graham](https://paulgraham.com/simply.html)
  Argues fancy language often conceals thin ideas, and that short sentences with common words force real clarity. Use for: catching yourself dressing up a weak point in complicated language.

### 2. Writing

- [Write Simply — Paul Graham](https://paulgraham.com/simply.html) (also above — central to both tracks 1 and 2)
- [Iceberg Theory — Ernest Hemingway (overview)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceberg_theory)
  "Theory of omission": state only the visible 1/8, trust the reader to feel the rest. Use for: cutting exposition and stage-direction from writing; a check against over-explaining.
- [BLUF (communication) — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BLUF_(communication))
  U.S. military writing standard (formalized in Army Regulation 25-50, 1988): open every memo/email with the conclusion before any background. Direct written-form cousin of the Pyramid Principle. Use for: emails, status updates, any message with a busy reader.
- [The Pyramid Principle Applied](https://managementconsulted.com/pyramid-principle/) (also above)
  Same structure applies directly to written memos/emails, not just spoken arguments.

### 3. Interpersonal communication

- [Chris Voss, the FBI Hostage Negotiator — Every](https://every.to/everything/chris-voss-the-fbi-hostage-negotiator-405720)
  Explains tactical empathy, mirroring (repeat back 1–3 key words) and labeling ("It sounds like...", "It seems like...") as ways to build rapport and de-escalate. Use for: any difficult or emotionally loaded conversation.
- [Active Listening in Negotiation — Academy of Negotiation](https://academyofnegotiation.com/blog-active-listening-in-negotiation)
  Broader grounding on active listening as a negotiation discipline, not just a soft skill. Use for: structured practice of listening technique, not just "paying attention."
- [Put Feelings Into Words and Ask Open-Ended Questions — Gottman Institute](https://www.gottman.com/blog/what-makes-love-last-putting-your-feelings-into-words-and-asking-open-ended-questions/)
  Grounds open questions ("what," "how," "describe...") vs. leading/closed ones, and warns against naming someone else's emotion for them ("Did that make you angry?" vs. "What came up for you?"). Use for: rapport-building and difficult conversations, alongside Voss's labeling.

### 4. Public speaking / stage presence

- [TED Talks: The Official TED Guide to Public Speaking — Chris Anderson (notes)](https://calvinrosser.com/notes/ted-talks-chris-anderson/)
  Core teaching: one talk = one idea, expressible as a ≤15-word throughline; script your first and last lines; use story to carry complex ideas. Use for: designing any talk structure, from a 5-minute update to a full keynote.
- [The Throughline concept — worldhatchlearning notes on the Official TED Guide](https://worldhatchlearning.wordpress.com/2017/08/17/whats-the-big-idea-every-talk-should-have-a-throughline-that-ties-everything-together-presentation-tools-from-the-official-ted-guide-the-throughline/)
  Worked examples of real 15-word throughlines from famous TED talks. Use for: the specific drill of compressing a talk idea into one sentence before building slides/content.
- [Speech Analysis: I Have a Dream — Martin Luther King Jr. (Six Minutes)](https://sixminutes.dlugan.com/speech-analysis-dream-martin-luther-king/)
  Close rhetorical read of anaphora ("I have a dream...", "Now is the time...") and how repetition builds memorability and momentum. Use for: studying rhythm and repetition as a deliberate device, not decoration.
- [Vocal Techniques: Tone, Pitch, Pace, and Pauses — Fiveable](https://fiveable.me/advanced-public-speaking/unit-6/vocal-techniques-tone-pitch-pace-pauses/study-guide/KG9dFBO6bneiYOPt)
  Names the specific pause types (dramatic, cognitive, emphatic) and when to use each. Use for: deliberate practice of pacing/pausing, not just "slow down."

### 5. On-camera presence

- [Eye Contact in Video Podcasting — Where to Actually Look](https://www.justtalkstudios.com/post/eye-contact-in-video-podcasting-where-to-actually-look)
  Concrete mechanics of lens-vs-screen eye contact for video. Use for: fixing the single most common on-camera "tell" (looking at yourself instead of the lens).
- [How to Instantly Improve Your On-Camera Presence — Master Your Presence](https://masteryourpresence.com/blog/how-to-instantly-improve-your-on-camera-presence-and-look-confident-in-any-interview)
  "Talk to one person, not an audience" framing for calibrating energy on camera vs. live. Use for: podcast/interview delivery, solo video framing.

### 6. Persuasion & rhetoric

- [Aristotle's Rhetorical Appeals: Ethos, Logos, and Pathos — Open Rhetoric (Pressbooks)](https://pressbooks.pub/openrhetoric/chapter/aristotles-rhetorical-appeals/)
  Direct grounding in Aristotle's *Rhetoric* — credibility, logic, emotion as the three levers of persuasion, and why logos alone rarely wins a real audience. Use for: diagnosing why an argument that's "technically right" isn't landing.
- [Chris Voss — tactical empathy & labeling (Every)](https://every.to/everything/chris-voss-the-fbi-hostage-negotiator-405720) (also above)
  Directly relevant to handling pushback/objections — label the objection before answering it.
- [The LAER Framework for objection handling — Highspot](https://www.highspot.com/blog/objection-handling/)
  Listen, Acknowledge, Explore, Respond — validate the objection as legitimate before offering any counter, and paraphrase it back to confirm you understood it correctly. Use for: any live pushback, not just sales — the sequence generalizes to Q&A and disagreements.

### 7. Non-verbal & vocal mastery

- [Vocal Techniques: Tone, Pitch, Pace, and Pauses — Fiveable](https://fiveable.me/advanced-public-speaking/unit-6/vocal-techniques-tone-pitch-pace-pauses/study-guide/KG9dFBO6bneiYOPt) (also above)
- [3 Vocal Presentation Techniques to Command the Room — Moxie Institute](https://www.moxieinstitute.com/vocal-presentation-techniques-thatcher-obama/)
  Case studies (Thatcher, Obama) of pitch-lowering and deliberate pacing to project authority. Use for: studying modern political speakers as delivery models, not just historical ones.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [Toastmasters International — Find a Club](https://www.toastmasters.org/find-a-club)
  Structured, low-stakes, real-room speaking practice with built-in feedback (evaluators) at every meeting; free to visit before joining. Use for: real-world reps on stage presence, impromptu speaking (Table Topics), and getting live feedback instead of self-assessment alone.
- [r/publicspeaking](https://www.reddit.com/r/publicspeaking/)
  ~16K members, active discussion and critique of speeches. Use for: crowd-sourced feedback on talk structure/delivery, seeing how others troubleshoot nerves and Q&A.
