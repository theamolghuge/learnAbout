# Mamba Mentality Resources

A deliberately small, tiered list — depth over coverage. Every claim in every lesson should trace back to something here, not to parametric memory. See [[MISSION.md]] for why the list is capped this way.

## Knowledge

### Tier 1 — Essential
The smallest set of primary sources that actually carries the curriculum. Read in this order.

- [Book: _Meditations_ — Marcus Aurelius, trans. Gregory Hays](https://www.amazon.com/Meditations-New-Translation-Marcus-Aurelius/dp/0812968255)
  Hays's translation is the modern standard — "the first in a generation" to give Marcus's private notebook real immediacy in English. Primary source, philosophical. **Extract:** the dichotomy of control (Stage 1, 3), the discipline of judgment (Stage 3, 8), and the daily-practice form itself — it's literally a man's private operating-system notes, which is the template for your eventual Stage 10 Manifesto. **Order:** read alongside Stage 1–3. **Skip:** nothing — it's short (under 250 pages) and every book is relevant.
- [Book: _The Bhagavad Gita_, trans. Eknath Easwaran](https://www.amazon.com/Bhagavad-Gita-2nd-Eknath-Easwaran/dp/1586380192)
  Easwaran translates in plain, unpretentious prose with a 55-page introduction placing each idea in context — avoids both academic density and New Age dilution. Primary source, philosophical. **Extract:** nishkama karma (action without attachment to outcome — Ch. 2–3) and dharma (Ch. 2, 18), the single most direct ancient answer to "mastery vs. validation." **Order:** read Ch. 2–3 for Stage 1–2; the rest supports later stages. **Skip:** nothing on a first pass, but don't try to absorb all 18 chapters' cosmology at once — the psychological/ethical core is in Ch. 2–3 and 18.
- [Book: _The Mamba Mentality: How I Play_ — Kobe Bryant](https://www.amazon.com/Mamba-Mentality-How-Play/dp/0374201234)
  Bryant's own account, published 2018 (MCD/Farrar, Straus and Giroux), with an introduction by Phil Jackson and foreword by Pau Gasol. Primary source, anecdotal/historical (his own testimony — treat as his self-account, not independently verified fact). **Extract:** his obsession with preparation and film study, his relationship to failure ("if I fail today, I'm going to learn something from that failure"), and the difference between playing to win vs. playing to figure things out. **Order:** central to Stage 5 (Mastery), useful throughout. **Skip:** the play-by-play breakdowns of specific NBA matchups if you're not a basketball tactics reader — the mental-approach chapters carry the curriculum.
- [Book: _Peak: Secrets from the New Science of Expertise_ — Anders Ericsson & Robert Pool](https://www.amazon.com/Peak-Secrets-New-Science-Expertise/dp/0544456238)
  Ericsson ran the original deliberate-practice research program (including the Berlin violinists study); this is the accessible synthesis, and it explicitly corrects Malcolm Gladwell's popularized "10,000-hour rule." Evidence-based. **Extract:** the actual definition of deliberate practice (specific goals, full attention, immediate feedback, pushing past your current level) vs. mere repetition. **Order:** Stage 5 (Mastery) foundation. **Skip:** none — it's the load-bearing psychology text for the whole "mastery" stage.
- [Book: _Man's Search for Meaning_ — Viktor Frankl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man's_Search_for_Meaning)
  Frankl, a psychiatrist and Auschwitz survivor, wrote this in nine days after liberation (1946); Part I is memoir, Part II introduces logotherapy. Primary source, historical + philosophical. **Extract:** meaning is found through work, love, or unavoidable suffering — and "everything can be taken from a man but one thing: the last of the human freedoms — to choose one's attitude in any given set of circumstances." **Order:** Stage 6 (Adversity), but worth reading early — it recalibrates what "adversity" even means before the curriculum uses the word loosely. **Skip:** nothing; it's under 200 pages.
- [Book: _Nicomachean Ethics_ — Aristotle, trans. Joe Sachs (Hackett)](https://hackettpublishing.com/nicomachean-ethics-sachs)
  Sachs's translation is praised specifically for pairing precision on Aristotle's Greek with plain, readable English — a real concern here, since this is the single most load-bearing text for Stage 2. Primary source, philosophical. **Extract:** arete (excellence/virtue) as a *hexis* — a stable disposition built by repeatedly doing the virtuous act, not a feeling or a one-time decision (Book II.1) — and eudaimonia ("flourishing," badly translated as "happiness") as activity in accordance with virtue across "a complete life," not a peak moment (Book I.7). **Order:** central to Stage 2. **Skip:** Books V–VII (justice, akrasia, the technical taxonomy of virtues) unless you want to go deeper than this curriculum requires — Books I–II carry the core claim.

### Tier 2 — Deepening
Read once Tier 1 has landed. Each fills a gap Tier 1 leaves open.

- [Book: _The Yoga Sūtras of Patañjali_, trans. Edwin F. Bryant](https://www.amazon.com/Yoga-Sutras-Pata%C3%B1jali-Translation-Commentary/dp/0865477361)
  Bryant (Ph.D., Indic Languages, Columbia; taught at Harvard and Rutgers) goes to the classical commentaries directly rather than filtering through a single modern lens. Primary source, philosophical. **Extract:** abhyasa (sustained practice) and vairagya (non-attachment) as the two pillars of mental discipline (Sutra 1.12), and tapas (disciplined effort/austerity) as a component of kriya yoga. **Order:** Stage 3 (Self-Mastery) and Stage 4 (Discipline). **Skip:** the deeply technical metaphysics of citta-vrtti in the later chapters unless you want to go further than this curriculum requires.
- [Book: _Grit: The Power of Passion and Perseverance_ — Angela Duckworth](https://www.amazon.com/Grit-Passion-Perseverance-Angela-Duckworth/dp/1501111112) · [Original paper (PDF)](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/personality/conscientiousness/2007-duckworth.pdf)
  The 2007 paper (Duckworth, Peterson, Matthews & Kelly, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*) introduced the Grit Scale; grit explained a modest but real ~4% of variance in outcomes across West Point retention, National Spelling Bee ranking, and Ivy League GPA. Evidence-based — read the actual effect size, not just the TED-talk version. **Extract:** grit as "an ultimate concern" that organizes effort over years, distinct from raw talent or momentary intensity. **Order:** Stage 4–5.
- [Book: _Mindset: The New Psychology of Success_ — Carol Dweck](https://www.amazon.com/Mindset-Psychology-Carol-S-Dweck/dp/0345472322)
  Built on Dweck's original praise research (Mueller & Dweck, 1998): praising *intelligence* pushes children toward a fixed mindset; praising *process* supports a growth mindset. Evidence-based, though popularized beyond the original findings — the common misreading is that "growth mindset" just means "try harder"; the actual research requires new strategies and feedback-seeking, not effort alone. **Extract:** how you interpret failure (Stage 6) is a learned disposition, not a fixed trait.
- [Book: _The Book of Five Rings_ — Miyamoto Musashi, trans. William Scott Wilson](https://www.shambhala.com/the-book-of-five-rings-2112.html)
  Wilson (the foremost English translator of samurai-era texts, also translated *Hagakure*) adheres closely to the 17th-century Japanese and clarifies ambiguities in older translations. Primary source, historical/philosophical. **Extract:** Musashi's insistence on studying your craft daily without exception ("the Way of walking alone") and strategy as something trained into the body, not merely thought about. **Order:** Stage 5, as a counterpoint to Ericsson's clinical framing — same idea (deliberate, obsessive practice), from a 17th-century swordsman instead of a modern lab.
- [Paper: "The Destructiveness of Perfectionism Revisited" — Flett & Hewitt et al. (2022), *Clinical Psychology Review*](https://hewittlab.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2023/01/Flett-Hewitt-et-al-SPP-Destructiveness.pdf)
  Hewitt and Flett built the field's multidimensional model of perfectionism (self-oriented, other-oriented, socially prescribed) and, notably, argue *against* the popular idea that any form of perfectionism is fully healthy. Evidence-based. **Extract:** the actual research distinction between high personal standards and perfectionism — load-bearing for Stage 8 (Character), where you're explicitly asked to tell these apart in your own life.
- [APA overview: Self-Determination Theory — Deci & Ryan](https://www.apa.org/research-practice/conduct-research/self-determination-theory)
  Deci and Ryan's decades-long research program (University of Rochester) on autonomy, competence, and relatedness as the three needs underlying intrinsic motivation. Evidence-based. **Extract:** "competence" — the intrinsic need to feel mastery — is itself a well-supported psychological need, not just an ancient philosophical claim; this is the modern evidence-based bridge to the Gita's case for practicing for its own sake (Stage 1–2).
- [_The Enchiridion_ — Epictetus, trans. Elizabeth Carter (public domain)](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html)
  Epictetus's short handbook is the source text for the "dichotomy of control" — the opening line ("Some things are up to us, and some are not") is the whole idea in one sentence. Public-domain Carter translation is serviceable and free; Robin Hard's modern translation is the recommended paid upgrade if the Carter prose feels dated. Primary source, philosophical. **Extract:** the up-to-us / not-up-to-us sort itself — Marcus Aurelius (Tier 1) is Epictetus's most famous reader and restates this idea constantly in *Meditations*, especially Book 8. **Order:** Stage 1 (responsibility) and Stage 3. **Skip:** it's ~15 pages; read the whole thing.
- [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Stoicism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/stoicism/)
  Academic reference, not a primary source — used specifically for the technical apparatus classical Stoic authors assume but don't always spell out. Evidence: historical/philosophical (accurately reports what the school held, doesn't argue for it). **Extract:** the doctrine that virtue alone is good and vice alone bad, with everything else — health, wealth, reputation, even life — classed as "indifferent" (*adiaphora*), further split into "preferred" and "dispreferred"; and the distinction between *selecting* a preferred indifferent (health over illness) and *choosing* virtue, which alone counts as good in the strict sense. **Order:** Stage 2, directly against Aristotle's claim that eudaimonia needs external goods.
- [Book: _The Dhammapada_, trans. Gil Fronsdal](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/56760/the-dhammapada-by-gil-fronsdal-foreword-by-jack-kornfield/)
  Deliberately **not** Easwaran's Dhammapada, despite using Easwaran for the Gita (Tier 1) — reviewers and Buddhist scholars note his Dhammapada is "amazingly free" with the Pali and reads as "Dhammapada according to Easwaran," softened toward Vedantic concepts foreign to the original. Fronsdal (Ph.D., Buddhist Studies; Pali scholar and practicing teacher) is reviewed as balancing genuine fidelity to the text with readability — the right call specifically because this lesson hinges on a doctrine (anatta, no-self) that a Hindu-inflected translation would be most likely to blur. Primary source, philosophical. **Extract:** the Twin Verses (Ch. 1) on mind and craving as the root of suffering. **Order:** Stage 2, as the third framework alongside Stoicism and Aristotle. **Skip:** nothing; it's short and aphoristic like the Gita and Meditations.
- [Paper: "Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal" — Balban, Neri, Kogon & Huberman (2023), *Cell Reports Medicine*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36630953/)
  Stanford RCT comparing three breathwork protocols against mindfulness meditation; cyclic sighing (extended exhale) beat box breathing and hyperventilation-style breathwork on reducing physiological arousal and improving mood, with effects measurable after a single session. Evidence-based. **Extract:** the cyclic-sighing technique itself (double inhale through the nose, long exhale through the mouth) as a body-first (not reasoning-first) regulation tool. **Order:** Stage 3, as the physiological complement to Stage 3's mental-discipline material — deliberately not a substitute for the cognitive/reframing techniques already covered in your [[anvikshiki]] workspace.
- [Book: _Good Habits, Bad Habits: The Science of Making Positive Changes That Stick_ — Wendy Wood](https://dornsife.usc.edu/wendy-wood/good-habits-bad-habits/)
  Wood (USC psychologist) synthesizes three decades of her own habit research. Evidence-based. **Extract:** ~43% of daily behavior is repeated in a stable context and executed with minimal deliberation — habit strength comes from consistent context cues (location, time of day, preceding action, emotional state, other people present) plus repetition plus reward, not from motivation or willpower. **Order:** Stage 4 (Discipline) — the direct evidence-based answer to "what do I do when motivation is gone."
- [Paper: "How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world" — Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle (2010), *European Journal of Social Psychology*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674)
  UCL study tracking 96 people building a daily habit over 12 weeks. Evidence-based. **Extract:** habit formation averaged 66 days but ranged 18–254 days (the popular "66 days" claim overstates certainty) — and, the more load-bearing finding, missing an occasional repetition did not materially impair the habit-formation process. **Order:** Stage 4, directly against the all-or-nothing "I broke my streak" narrative.
- [Marlatt, G.A. & Gordon, J.R. (1985), _Relapse Prevention: Maintenance Strategies in the Treatment of Addictive Behaviors_](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6760427/)
  Foundational relapse-prevention model from addiction treatment research. Evidence-based, but scope-limited — developed for substance use, not general habit-building; treat the transfer to ordinary missed-practice-day situations as a borrowed mechanism, not a direct equivalence. **Extract:** the abstinence violation effect — guilt and self-judgment after a lapse, more than the lapse itself, predicts whether it becomes full abandonment. **Order:** Stage 4, paired with the Lally et al. finding above.
- [Ericsson, K.A., Krampe, R.T. & Tesch-Römer, C. (1993), "The Role of Deliberate Practice in the Acquisition of Expert Performance," *Psychological Review*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6731745/) · [2019 replication — Macnamara & Maitra, *Royal Society Open Science*](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rsos.190327)
  The original Berlin violinists study behind the "10,000 hours" claim, read together with its 2019 replication. Evidence-based. **Extract:** the original found accumulated deliberate practice explained ~48% of skill variance; the tighter, double-blind replication found ~26% and no significant practice-time difference between the "best" and "good" tiers — a real, substantial, but incomplete effect. **Order:** Stage 5 opener, directly against the popular "10,000 hours" oversimplification (which *Peak*, Tier 1, already partially corrects — this goes further, into the original study's own limits).
- [Tedeschi, R.G. & Calhoun, L.G. (1996), "The Posttraumatic Growth Inventory," *Journal of Traumatic Stress*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/jts.2490090305) · [critical review — Jayawickreme et al., *Current Directions in Psychological Science* (2019)](https://scottbarrykaufman.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Fixing-the-Growth-Illusion-New-Directions-for-Research-in-Resilience-and-Posttraumatic-Growth.pdf)
  Read the two together, deliberately. Evidence-based, but read the limitations first. **Extract:** post-traumatic growth is real for some people under some conditions (social support, active meaning-making) but is not automatic, not guaranteed, and its primary measurement instrument (the PTGI) captures self-perceived growth rather than verified change — a real methodological limitation, not a minor caveat. **Order:** Stage 6, directly against the popular "what doesn't kill you makes you stronger" oversimplification.
- Brickman, P. & Campbell, D. (1971) on hedonic adaptation; Brickman, Coates & Janoff-Bulman (1978), "Lottery Winners and Accident Victims: Is Happiness Relative?", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*. Ben-Shahar, T., *Happier* (Tier 3, secondary/popularization of the research).
  Evidence-based. **Extract:** people drift back toward a stable happiness baseline after both major positive and negative life changes — the "arrival fallacy" (Ben-Shahar) that reaching a goal produces lasting happiness is empirically false. **Order:** Stage 6 extension — the missing "handling success" counterpart to Lesson 18's failure review.
- [Beilock, S. & Carr, T. (2001), "On the Fragility of Skilled Performance: What Governs Choking Under Pressure?" *JEP: General*](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/xge-1304701.pdf) · [Beilock et al. (2011), "Choking Under Pressure: Multiple Routes to Skill Failure," *JEP: General*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21574739/)
  Evidence-based. **Extract:** explicit monitoring (over-attending to automated mechanics) disrupts well-learned motor skills under pressure; distraction (task-irrelevant worry) disrupts cognitively demanding tasks — different mechanisms needing different fixes. Self-consciousness training (practicing under simulated pressure/observation) eliminated choking in her putting experiments; dual-task training didn't. **Order:** Stage 7 opener.
- [Festinger, L. (1954), "A Theory of Social Comparison Processes," *Human Relations*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/001872675400700202)
  Evidence-based, foundational. **Extract:** social comparison is a basic drive, not an optional habit to eliminate — the lever is what's extracted from a comparison (specific, actionable information vs. a global verdict about self-worth), not whether comparison happens at all. **Order:** Stage 7, applying Stage 1's mastery/validation axis to competition specifically.
- [Schaufeli, W. et al., "The differences between workaholism and work engagement" (PDF)](https://www.wilmarschaufeli.nl/publications/Schaufeli/327.pdf)
  Evidence-based. **Extract:** work engagement (vigor, dedication, absorption) and workaholism overlap heavily on hours and effort; the discriminating factor is compulsion — engaged people work hard because they enjoy it, workaholics because of an inner compulsion they can't resist regardless of enjoyment. **Order:** Stage 8 opener, directly against romanticizing extreme effort without asking what's actually driving it.
- [Kernis, M.H. (2003), "Toward a Conceptualization of Optimal Self-Esteem," *Psychological Inquiry*](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/2003_Kernis_Optimal.pdf)
  Evidence-based. **Extract:** secure vs. fragile high self-esteem look identical from outside but differ on stability and contingency (dependence on continued achievement/approval) — with verbal defensiveness under challenge as a measurable marker separating them. **Order:** Stage 8, confidence vs. ego.
- ["Dear Basketball" (2017 short film, dir. Glen Keane) and Kobe's 2015 retirement letter it's based on](https://newsletter.oscars.org/news/post/remembering-kobe-bryant-and-his-oscar-winning-short-film-dear-basketball)
  Historical/anecdotal, well documented (first NBA player to win an Academy Award, 2018 Best Animated Short). **Extract:** a deliberate second act built from zero prior mastery, plus Granity Studios' name ("greater than infinity") as his own stated ambition for it. **Order:** Stage 9 opener.

### Tier 3 — Optional
Only if you want to go further than the core arc requires.

- [Locke, E. & Latham, G. (2002), "Building a Practically Useful Theory of Goal Setting and Task Motivation," *American Psychologist*](https://med.stanford.edu/content/dam/sm/s-spire/documents/PD.locke-and-latham-retrospective_Paper.pdf)
  Evidence-based, ~400 studies over four decades. **Extract:** specific, difficult goals reliably outperform vague or easy ones; self-efficacy (Bandura's task-specific belief in one's own capability, distinct from general confidence) determines goal commitment, response to setbacks, and strategy development. **Order:** Stage 4 extension.
- [Watts, T., Duncan, G. & Quan, H. (2018), "Revisiting the Marshmallow Test," *Psychological Science*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0956797618761661) · [Duckworth's 2019 methodological response](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6335313/)
  Evidence-based, genuinely contested. **Extract:** a ~10x larger, more diverse replication of Mischel's marshmallow test found the original correlation overstated — roughly halved before controls, reduced by two-thirds after controlling for family background and early cognitive ability — though Duckworth and others argue those controls may partly capture self-control itself. Both sides agree the popular version overstated the finding. **Order:** Stage 4 extension.
- [Mah, C. et al. (2011), "The Effects of Sleep Extension on the Athletic Performance of Collegiate Basketball Players," *Sleep*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21731144/) · [Yoo, S. et al. on sleep deprivation and amygdala reactivity, *Journal of Neuroscience*/*Current Biology*](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/31/12/4466)
  Evidence-based. **Extract:** extending sleep toward 10 hours/night measurably improved Stanford basketball players' sprint times and shooting accuracy; separately, sleep deprivation amplifies amygdala reactivity and weakens prefrontal-amygdala connectivity, undermining emotional regulation. **Order:** Stage 3 extension — closes the "recovery" gap from your original brief.
- ["Kobe Bryant's leadership doctrine" — ESPN Los Angeles Lakers Blog](https://www.espn.com/blog/los-angeles/lakers/post/_/id/33473/kobe-bryants-leadership-doctrine) and reporting on his Italian childhood (ages 6–13, father Joe Bryant's professional career there) and the documented 2008–09 shift in his approach to teammates
  Anecdotal/historical. **Extract:** the early-career "toxic culture" reputation (talent avoiding the Lakers because of it) alongside the documented later shift toward genuine connection and communication — a fuller, less flattering, more useful account than the popular version. **Order:** Stage 8 extension.
- [Roediger, H. & Karpicke, J. (2006), "The Power of Testing Memory," *Perspectives on Psychological Science*](http://psychnet.wustl.edu/memory/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Roediger-Karpicke-2006_PPS.pdf) · [Dunlosky, J. et al. (2013), "Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques," *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*]
  Evidence-based. **Extract:** the testing effect — retrieval practice produces significantly better long-term retention than rereading (61% vs. 40% after one week in the original study); testing and spacing are the only two of ten common study techniques rated "high utility" in Dunlosky's review. **Order:** Stage 5 extension — also names this workspace's own journal-prompt design choice explicitly.
- Six case-study profiles (Ramanujan, Kalam, Tendulkar, Feynman, Beethoven, Munger) — full citations for each are in <a href="../reference/case-studies.html">reference/case-studies.html</a>'s own sourcing; representative sources include Britannica biographies, G.H. Hardy's own account of Ramanujan, the Heiligenstadt Testament (public domain, 1802), and Feynman's own essay collection *The Pleasure of Finding Things Out*.
  Anecdotal/historical, verified via web search before writing rather than parametric memory. **Extract:** see Lesson 30 and the reference doc. **Order:** Stage 5 extension — fills the case-study breadth (science, business, art, and Indian figures specifically) your original brief asked for in Section 4.

- ["Athletic trainer recounts a crazy Kobe Bryant work ethic story in preparation for the 2008 Olympics" — Basketball Network](https://www.basketballnetwork.net/old-school/athletic-trainer-recounts-a-crazy-kobe-bryant-work-ethic-story-in-preparation-for-the-2008-olympics)
  Anecdotal, but multiply corroborated — Chris Bosh and Dwyane Wade independently described the same period publicly (Bosh: Kobe arriving to breakfast "with ice on his knees"; Wade: "he's already three hours and a full workout into his day"). Deliberately used in place of the vaguer, unattributed "Kobe always worked out at 4am" internet legend. **Extract:** one specific, verifiable instance of unwitnessed effort, not a general claim about his whole career. **Order:** Stage 5, as a case study — paired explicitly with an open question Stage 8 revisits rather than a resolved verdict.
- ESPN SportsCenter interview with Dave McMenamin (Kobe's "it's probably not a good idea" quote) and the 2015 documentary *Muse* (the "this could really be a wrap" quote), on the April 2013 Achilles tear
  Anecdotal, historical, directly from Kobe himself in both cases — one in-the-moment, one reflective. **Extract:** the honest doubt named in real time, and the fear admitted afterward — a more textured account than the highlight-reel version. **Order:** Stage 6, paired with the "pain tolerance vs. ignoring legitimate limits" distinction from your original brief.
- [Book: _Tao Te Ching_, trans. Stephen Mitchell](https://www.amazon.com/Tao-Te-Ching-Stephen-Mitchell/dp/0061142662)
  Philosophical, poetic rather than literal (Mitchell doesn't read classical Chinese; treat as an interpretive rendering, not a scholarly translation). **Extract:** wu wei ("effortless action") as a genuine counterpoint to Stoic willed effort and karma yoga's disciplined action — worth reading precisely because it *doesn't* agree with the other traditions, per your ask to be honest about disagreement. Used in Lesson 34.
- Plato's *Apology* and early definitional dialogues (*Euthyphro*, *Meno*) — public domain, widely available
  Primary source, philosophical/historical (Socrates wrote nothing; known entirely through Plato). **Extract:** the elenchus (question-driven unraveling of a confident definition, ending in aporia rather than a supplied answer) and "the unexamined life is not worth living" (Apology) — the method this workspace's own confront callouts have borrowed throughout. **Order:** Stage 2 extension.
- [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Japanese Zen Buddhist Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/japanese-zen/)
  Academic reference. **Extract:** Zen's distinct epistemological stance (direct experience/satori over scriptural study) and mushin ("no-mind") — its genuine convergence with Beilock's modern explicit-monitoring research (Stage 7). **Order:** Stage 2 extension, Lesson 34.
- [Book: _Can't Hurt Me_ — David Goggins](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/41721428-can-t-hurt-me) — read alongside [critical review: "Why Can't Hurt Me Fails Ambitious Men"](https://thepowermoves.com/cant-hurt-me-david-goggins/)
  Anecdotal, first-person. Include this one specifically **for Stage 8 (the dark side)**, not as an aspirational text: critics and Psychology Today analyses of Goggins's approach describe a pattern of "toxic resilience" — ignoring emotional needs for performance — and unresolved trauma redirected into endless physical punishment. Useful as a real case study of pain tolerance crossing into ignoring legitimate limits, one of the exact distinctions Stage 8 asks you to draw.
- [Book: _Mastery_ — Robert Greene](https://www.amazon.com/Mastery-Robert-Greene/dp/014312417X)
  Secondary source — a historical/anecdotal synthesis across many masters (da Vinci, Einstein, Musashi, and others), not a primary source or an evidence-based text. Useful only if you want broader biographical range than the case studies built into individual lessons; skip if Tier 1–2 already feels sufficiently grounded.
