# Mission: Mamba Mentality — A Philosophy of Excellence

## Why
You want the ideas underneath "Mamba Mentality" — relentless mastery, discipline without motivation, taking full responsibility, competing without being consumed by comparison, turning pain into fuel — to become **default behavior**, not a feeling you get from reading something good. The concrete test: what you actually do on a day you don't feel like doing it, how you actually respond the next time you fail publicly or get criticized, and what you actually choose to spend the next five years of deliberate effort on. If those three things haven't changed, this curriculum hasn't worked, no matter how much you've read.

## Success looks like
- On a day with zero motivation, you have a specific, rehearsed answer for what you do instead of skipping — not willpower, a system.
- After a real failure or public criticism, your first move is a structured review, not a spiral of self-judgment or a defensive dismissal of the feedback.
- You can state, in your own words, what you control and what you don't in a situation that's currently stressing you — and act only on the first half.
- You can name your own "vital few" — what deserves years of deliberate practice — and give the specific reason you're declining everything else, not just "I'm busy."
- You can articulate the difference between discipline and compulsion, confidence and ego, high standards and perfectionism — and can point to a real moment in your own life on each side of at least one of those lines.
- You have a personal, reread-able statement of principles (the eventual "Manifesto," Stage 10) that you actually go back to, not one you wrote once and forgot.
- When asked why you want to become exceptional, your answer holds up under a follow-up "why" — it isn't just "to prove something" dressed up in better language.

## Constraints
- **Primary sources over commentary, wherever one exists.** Kobe's own book over a summary of it; Easwaran's Gita over a paraphrase of the Gita; Hays's Marcus Aurelius over a listicle of Stoic quotes. See [[RESOURCES.md]].
- **Small, tiered resource list, not a library.** You explicitly don't want "100 books to look everywhere." [[RESOURCES.md]] is capped and tiered (Essential / Deepening / Optional) — depth over coverage.
- **Every knowledge claim is labeled by kind**: evidence-based (cites the actual study), philosophical (a tradition's claim, not a fact), historical (what's actually documented about a person), or anecdotal (a story, useful but not proof). Lessons don't blur these.
- **The dark side gets equal weight, not a disclaimer paragraph.** Discipline vs. compulsion, confidence vs. ego, sacrifice vs. self-destruction are treated as their own material, with real case studies of what went wrong — not a footnote after the inspiring part.
- **Confrontation over flattery.** Every lesson should include at least one question that's uncomfortable to answer honestly, not just information to receive.
- **This is a multi-year curriculum**, paced in the ten stages below. One tightly-scoped lesson at a time, in your zone of proximal development — not an attempt to deliver the whole thing in one sitting.

## Out of scope
- **Basketball skill instruction.** This is about the mentality, not shooting form or footwork.
- **Exhaustive biography of every person mentioned.** Figures (Jordan, Ramanujan, Musashi, etc.) are used as case studies for a specific principle, not covered comprehensively — depth on the lesson they teach, not their whole life story.
- **Becoming the thing this curriculum explicitly warns against.** If a lesson starts producing workaholism, self-worth tied entirely to output, or contempt for people who work differently, that's a failure of this mission, not a sign it's working.
- **Adopting Hindu/Buddhist/yogic practice as religious observance.** The Gita, Yoga Sutras, and Buddhist material are studied for their psychological and practical content for a modern secular reader — as you asked — not as spiritual practice or belief adoption.
- **Overlap with [[anvikshiki]].** That workspace already covers reasoning, bias-correction, and decision method (classical Indian logic + modern cognitive science). This workspace covers character and excellence. Where they'd naturally touch (e.g., self-inquiry, detachment from a belief), cross-link rather than re-teach.

## The arc
Ten stages, built one lesson at a time across many sessions:

1. **Awakening** — why pursue excellence at all; mastery vs. validation
2. **Philosophy** — what a good, meaningful life looks like (Stoicism, Gita, Aristotle, Buddhism — where they agree and disagree)
3. **Self-Mastery** — control of mind, emotion, and impulse (yoga philosophy, CBT, emotional regulation)
4. **Discipline** — doing the work when motivation is absent (habit science, tapas, systems over willpower)
5. **Mastery** — becoming exceptional at a craft (deliberate practice, Kobe's preparation, Musashi)
6. **Adversity** — getting stronger through failure and suffering (Frankl, resilience research, Kobe's response to setbacks)
7. **Performance** — executing under pressure (stress physiology, flow, competing without comparison)
8. **Character** — who you become while pursuing excellence (the dark side: discipline vs. compulsion, confidence vs. ego)
9. **Purpose** — what the excellence is ultimately for (dharma, legacy, Kobe's second act as storyteller/mentor)
10. **Integration** — turning all of it into a lifelong operating system (the personal Manifesto)

Stages build on each other — expect early sessions to stay in Awakening/Philosophy/Self-Mastery before the curriculum earns its way to Performance and Character.
