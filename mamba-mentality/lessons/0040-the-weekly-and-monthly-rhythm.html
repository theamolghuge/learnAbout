The Weekly and Monthly Rhythm

A daily practice got built in Lesson 9. Decades don't run on daily alone.

Extends Stage 10, closing the last structural gap: your original brief asked for daily, weekly, and monthly practices, plus "ways to maintain the philosophy for decades." Only the daily layer got built.

Lesson 9 gave you a daily practice. What's been missing is the layer above it — the checkpoints that catch what a single day's practice can't see: whether the daily habit is quietly drifting, whether last week had a setback or a win that never got actually processed, whether the Manifesto you wrote in Lesson 29 still describes someone real six months later, or a version of you that's moved on without updating the document.

Weekly: a short structured check

Once a week, run three quick questions — not a long session, five minutes is enough: Did anything happen this week that deserves Lesson 18's failure-review protocol or Lesson 37's success-review, and did you actually run it, or did it just pass unprocessed? Where are you, honestly, on the Dark Side Checklist right now — closer to the healthy side or the corrosive one, on any row? Is your Lesson 9 daily practice's cue (Lesson 10) still firing reliably, or has it quietly gone stale?

Monthly: reread the Manifesto

Once a month, reread Lesson 29's Manifesto in full, out loud if you can. Revise anything that no longer feels true rather than leaving it as a record of who you were when you wrote it. A Manifesto that never changes for years is either a rare case of genuinely settled conviction, or a document nobody's actually checking against reality anymore — worth being honest with yourself about which one it is.

Why this is what "decades" actually requires Lesson 15 gave you Musashi's timeframe — a thousand days to forge, ten thousand to refine. No single day's motivation survives that span intact; per Lesson 10's own research, nothing that relies on sustained motivation does. What actually survives a multi-year timeframe is a system with scheduled correction points — weekly enough to catch drift before it compounds, monthly enough to catch whether the whole direction still makes sense. This rhythm is that system, not a new idea on top of everything else.

Schedule it now — not "when it feels right"

Per Lesson 10, a practice without a real context cue doesn't survive a normal week. This one gets the same treatment as Lesson 9's daily practice.
