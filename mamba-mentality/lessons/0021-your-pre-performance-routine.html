Your Pre-Performance Routine

Three separate levers. Elite performers don't leave any of them to chance in the moment.

Closes Stage 7. Assembles Stage 3's physiological reset, Lesson 19's attention placement, and Lesson 20's comparison handling into one sequence you run before the moment arrives — not during it, when it's hardest to think clearly at all.

Three separate mechanisms have come up for performing under pressure, from three different research traditions: cyclic sighing to regulate the body's arousal (Stage 3), trusting automaticity and keeping attention external rather than on your own mechanics (Lesson 19), and converting comparison into extractable information instead of a verdict (Lesson 20). None of them work reliably if you're trying to remember and apply them for the first time in the moment itself — that's also, not coincidentally, exactly the challenge-skill balance and flow research already covered in your attention workspace: the conditions for good performance are mostly set up beforehand, not fixed in the middle of it.

Build the actual sequence

A pre-performance routine is just these three levers, in a fixed order, run every time before a specific high-stakes moment — so by the time the moment arrives, the levers are already engaged.
