# Game Theory Resources

Knowledge for lessons should be drawn from here first, not from unchecked parametric memory. See [[MISSION.md]] for why this workspace exists.

## Knowledge

- [Open Yale Courses — ECON 159: Game Theory, Ben Polak](https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-159)
  Full, free video course (24 lectures). The best "sit and watch" primary source for this workspace — Polak teaches from first principles with the same intuition-before-formalism philosophy this workspace uses, and every lecture is built around a real example (dating, dating auctions, arms races, business). Use for: any lesson where you want the fuller derivation behind a concept taught here in compressed form.
- [Game Theory 101, William Spaniel](https://gametheory101.com/) · [YouTube playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL102B69CCA6049B6C)
  Short, sharply-scoped videos (5-15 min each) that isolate one concept at a time — strict dominance, mixed Nash, subgame perfection, Bayesian games. Use for: a quick refresher on one specific mechanic after a lesson here, rather than a full lecture.
- Book: _The Art of Strategy_ — Avinash Dixit & Barry Nalebuff
  The standard "game theory for smart non-mathematicians" book, written by two of the field's best-known applied theorists. Business- and life-example-heavy, almost no notation. Use for: the negotiation, business-competition, and everyday-life sections of this workspace — it's the closest existing book to this workspace's own brief.
- Book: _The Strategy of Conflict_ — Thomas Schelling
  The original text on commitment, credible threats, and focal points — still the sharpest treatment of why *tying your own hands* can be a source of power, not weakness. Use for: sequential games, deterrence, and negotiation lessons.
- [Stanford Online / Coursera — Game Theory (Jackson, Leyton-Brown, Shoham)](https://online.stanford.edu/courses/soe-ycs0002-game-theory) and [Game Theory II: Advanced Applications](https://online.stanford.edu/courses/soe-ycs0004-game-theory-ii-advanced-applications)
  More formal than Polak's course — covers Bayesian games, mechanism design, and auctions in more depth. Use for: the later, more advanced sections of this workspace (mechanism design, auctions) once foundations are solid.
- [PON — Program on Negotiation at Harvard Law School](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/)
  Harvard's negotiation research center; free articles and reports on BATNA, ZOPA, anchoring, and real negotiation case studies, written for practitioners rather than academics. Use for: every negotiation lesson — it's the highest-trust practical source on that specific subtopic.

## Wisdom (Communities)

### Gap — no strong dedicated community identified yet
Game theory doesn't have an obvious high-signal public community the way, say, a fitness or programming topic would — r/gametheory on Reddit is dominated by unrelated video-content discussion, not the field. The realistic path to "wisdom" here is less about joining a forum and more about testing the framework on your own live situations (negotiations, career decisions) and bringing the results back to lessons — which the mission already commits to. If you want a forum anyway:
- [r/AskEconomics](https://reddit.com/r/AskEconomics) — heavily moderated, academically-grounded; can field specific game-theory questions but isn't a dedicated community for the topic.
- Local/professional: any negotiation-specific workshop tied to your work (many companies run internal negotiation training) would be a higher-signal "wisdom" source than an online forum, if one is ever available to you.

This gap should be revisited if a better-fit community turns up.
